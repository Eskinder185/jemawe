Overview

The Ethiopian Community Association in Atlanta (ECAA) needed a single, trustworthy home online for its programs, membership, events, leadership, and documents. The site is designed mobile-first, because most community members reach it from a phone.

The opportunity

Community information was spread across places that were hard to keep current. The association runs on volunteers, so any solution had to let non-technical people update the most time-sensitive content, such as events and the home page, without asking a developer.

Objectives

  • Present the association, its programs, and how to join on a fast, mobile-friendly site.
  • Make events, home page features, and leadership listings editable by volunteers.
  • Keep content in a simple structured format that is easy to back up and review.

The solution

A React and Vite website styled with Tailwind CSS, with content stored as structured JSON files. A Decap CMS editor at /admin lets editors change three areas: the home page, events and news, and leadership. Other pages stay code-managed so the structure remains tidy. Membership registration links to the association's existing Jotform form rather than rebuilding it.

Features delivered

  • Ten sections: Home, About, Programs and Services, Membership, Events and News, Leadership, Documents, Media, Support ECAA, and Contact.
  • An English and Amharic language switch in the header.
  • Curated home page sections editors can show or hide without deleting content.
  • Event lists by status: upcoming, announcements, community news, and past events.
  • Leadership listings grouped by committee, editable in the CMS.
  • Image uploads saved into the site's own uploads folder.

Design and development decisions

  • Start the editor small. Only three content areas are editable at first, which keeps training simple and the rest of the site consistent.
  • Hide, do not delete. "Show this item?" switches let editors retire content safely.
  • Verify before publishing. Editors are guided not to invent names or roles and to mark unconfirmed details.
