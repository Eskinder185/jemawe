Give it an owner

Decide who is responsible for computer upkeep, even if it is a part-time task or an outside provider. Without an owner, updates and backups are everyone's job, which means nobody's.

Schedule updates

Turn on automatic updates for operating systems and browsers, and agree a time each week when computers are restarted to finish installing them. Unplanned restarts during the working day are what make people postpone updates indefinitely.

Back up, then test the backup

Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of important data, on two different types of storage, with one copy kept away from the office. Once a quarter, restore a file to prove the backup actually works.

Protect accounts

  • Use a password manager so every account can have a long, unique password.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in for email and any service that holds customer data.
  • Give each person their own account; avoid shared logins.
  • Remove access promptly when someone leaves.

Keep a device inventory

A simple spreadsheet is enough: device, owner, model, purchase date, warranty end, and operating system. It tells you which machines are ageing out and makes insurance claims and replacements far easier.

Plan replacements before they fail

Note when each computer will stop receiving security updates for its operating system. Budget for replacements ahead of that date, and consider upgrades for machines that are still supported but slow.

Write it down

Put the routine on one page: what happens weekly, monthly, and yearly, and who does it. Our monthly maintenance routine is a good starting point for each computer.
