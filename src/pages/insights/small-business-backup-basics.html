The rule

  • 3 copies of your important data: the original plus two backups.
  • 2 different types of storage, for example the computer itself and an external drive, so one failure does not take everything.
  • 1 copy off-site, such as a cloud backup service or a drive kept at another location, in case of theft, fire, or flood.

What to back up

Customer records, financial files, contracts, email, photos of your work, and anything you would have to recreate by hand. Software can be reinstalled; your data cannot.

How often

Back up as often as you can afford to lose work. If losing a day's work would hurt, back up daily. Automatic backups are far more reliable than remembering to do it.

Test the restore

A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a plan. Every few months, restore a few files from each backup to a test folder and open them.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping the backup drive permanently plugged in next to the computer, where the same accident or malware can reach both.
  • Assuming a file-syncing service is a backup. Syncing copies deletions and changes too; use a service with version history.
  • Backing up one computer but not the others.
