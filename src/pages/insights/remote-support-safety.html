Warning signs of a scam

  • You were contacted first. Real technology companies do not call out of the blue to say your computer has a virus.
  • A pop-up tells you to call a number. Genuine security warnings do not ask you to phone for help or lock your screen with a phone number.
  • They create urgency. "Your bank account is at risk" or "your computer will be disabled today".
  • They want unusual payment. Gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency are major red flags.
  • They ask for passwords or bank logins. A support technician never needs them.

What genuine remote support looks like

  • You contacted the provider first, through a website or number you found yourself.
  • The session is scheduled, and you start it by approving a connection on your own screen.
  • You can see everything the technician does and end the session at any time.
  • Nobody asks for your passwords; if one is needed, you type it yourself.
  • The cost is agreed before work starts, and paid through normal methods.

These are the rules every Jemawe remote session follows.

If you already let someone in

  1. Disconnect the computer from the internet straight away.
  2. Run a full scan with your security software.
  3. From a different, trusted device, change the passwords for email, banking, and important accounts.
  4. Contact your bank if you shared payment details or made a payment.
  5. Report the scam to your country's consumer protection or fraud reporting agency.
